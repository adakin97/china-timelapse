About this map

Each frame is light seen from orbit at night. Press play, or drag the slider, to watch thirty years of building, electrification and urban growth, and the high-speed rail network appearing from 2003. Built-up land, metro systems and airports will follow.

What the numbers mean

"Brightly lit area" is the land inside the border drawn here that shines at city or town strength (20 or more on the data's scale of 0 to 63). It counts how far strong light has spread, not how bright the cities have become.

"High-speed rail" is the length of line open by that year, estimated from the mapped tracks. It is approximate; see the limits below.

Data

  • Night lights: Li, Zhou, Zhao and Zhao (2020), A harmonized global nighttime light dataset 1992-2018, Scientific Data 7:168, with the authors' extension to 2021. It joins two generations of satellite sensor (DMSP until 2013, VIIRS from 2014) into one comparable series at about 1 km. Accessed through Google Earth Engine.
  • High-speed rail: © OpenStreetMap contributors, under the Open Database Licence. Every track tagged as high-speed (which in OpenStreetMap includes lines of 200 km/h and above), with the opening date its mappers recorded.
  • Borders: the Aliyun DataV boundary service.
  • Coastlines: Natural Earth.

Limits

  • Each frame is a three-year average centred on its year. The raw yearly pictures flicker, because different satellites flew in different years and their readings do not agree exactly.
  • The two sensors do not see alike. Faint light is left out because the newer sensor picks up glow the older one could not, and a small step can remain around 2014.
  • The older sensor saturates in city centres, so the brightest cores look the same from year to year even when they grew brighter.
  • Light is a proxy. Gas flares, fishing fleets and snow glare register too; dimming can mean better-shielded lamps as well as decline.
  • Rail opening years come from volunteers' tags. Where a track had none, it takes the year of the nearest dated track on the same line. Some years will be wrong, and the length is an estimate, not an official figure.

Borders

Boundaries follow China's official standard map. Several are disputed, and their use here is a design choice for a map about China, not a statement on those disputes.
