China Timelapse

The country seen from space at night, one picture a year from 1992 to 2021.

1992

About this map

Each frame is one year of light seen from orbit at night. Press play, or drag the slider, to watch thirty years of building, electrification and urban growth. This is the first layer of a larger project; built-up land, high-speed rail, metro systems and airports will follow.

What the number means

"Brightly lit area" is the land inside the border drawn here that shines at city or town strength (20 or more on the data's scale of 0 to 63). It counts how far strong light has spread, not how bright the cities have become. The dip in 2014 is the change of satellite sensor, not a real darkening.

Data

  • Night lights: Li, Zhou, Zhao and Zhao (2020), A harmonized global nighttime light dataset 1992-2018, Scientific Data 7:168, with the authors' extension to 2021. It joins two generations of satellite sensor (DMSP until 2013, VIIRS from 2014) into one comparable series at about 1 km. Accessed through Google Earth Engine.
  • Borders: the Aliyun DataV boundary service.
  • Coastlines: Natural Earth.

Limits

  • The two sensors do not see alike. The join between 2013 and 2014 is corrected in the data but can still show as a small step.
  • The older sensor saturates in city centres, so the brightest cores look the same from year to year even when they grew brighter.
  • Light is a proxy. Gas flares, fishing fleets and snow glare register too; dimming can mean better-shielded lamps as well as decline.

Borders

Boundaries follow China's official standard map. Several are disputed, and their use here is a design choice for a map about China, not a statement on those disputes.
